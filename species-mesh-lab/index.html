Lindheimer’s muhly — design notes

Broad fountain habit, dense basal tuft, folded blue-green tapering blades and tall slender culms with densely flowered narrow panicles with shorter, ascending branches and a slightly more open mature appearance. Varied blade curvature, thickness, twist and muted dry foliage. Sources: Wildflower Center · Grass ID Lab record (Hagenbuch & Lemke 2015 p.54; Diggs et al. 1999 pp.1286,1288). Ligules and microscopic hairs omitted.

Little bluestem — design notes

Upright bunch with fuller alternating leaves along lower and middle culms, variable stem angles, short separate racemes terminating leafy branches, paired spikelets and more visible silky hairs. Leafy growth includes nonflowering shoots; fall and winter show copper, reddish bronze and straw tones. Fine structures are enlarged selectively for landscape readability. Sources: Wildflower Center · Grass ID Lab record (Hagenbuch & Lemke 2015 p.60; Diggs et al. 1999 p.1316; Gould 1975 pp.608–609). Racemes use approximate 2.5–5 cm lengths; hair and awn geometry is stylized. No particular variety or cultivar is claimed.

Autumn sage — design notes

Fuller irregular woody mound with foliage on interior and lower branches, tapered brown branches and green axillary shoots, opposite pairs of rounded elliptic to obovate leaves, variable terminal racemes with paired flowers, and red tubular corollas with a hood and distinct three-lobed lower lip. Leaf and flower geometry is selectively stylized for landscape readability. Mild winter retains much of the foliage; persistent dry calyces are illustrative, not verified fruit. No cultivar or local genotype claim. Sources: Wildflower Center · A. C. Gibson, Vascular Plants of Williamson County. Existing palette deciduous flag conflicts with the primary profile; the palette has not been changed.

Fragrant mistflower — design notes

Rounded multistem shrub with opposite pointed, coarsely toothed leaves, three main veins and compact, gently domed terminal bouquets held beyond the foliage on outward shoots, with several short branching sprays carrying tightly grouped heads of pinkish-white disk florets and projecting styles; no ray florets. Mild winter can retain foliage; dry bristled fruit structures are illustrative. Fine floral geometry is simplified and enlarged selectively. Sources: Wildflower Center · Texas Wildbuds floral details. Existing palette deciduous flag is not changed.

Texas mountain laurel — design notes

Multitrunk evergreen small tree, curved tapered woody limbs, alternate odd-pinnate leaves with seven or nine rounded leaflets, fuller compound foliage sprays, densely flowered hanging lavender pea-flower racemes, and gray-brown constricted pods in fall and winter. Leaflet surfaces, flower proportions and pods are stylized; winter foliage stays green. Sources: Wildflower Center · Oregon State University · Texas Wildbuds.

Red yucca — design notes

Clumping evergreen basal rosettes, arching narrow grooved leaves with curling marginal fibers, tall branching pink stalks and coral-colored tubular flowers with six stylized tepals; rounded capsules mature green to brown. Winter retains plum-tinted foliage. Sources: Wildflower Center · Oregon State University. No cultivar or county-level nativity claim.

Texas frogfruit — design notes

Low, irregular spreading mat with connected creeping stems, axillary branches and opposite, wedge-based leaves toothed toward the tips. Small purple-bracted headlike spikes with pale pink-white flowers are carried on ascending axillary peduncles. Semi-evergreen; winter thinning and browning are illustrative. Fine structures are selectively enlarged at lower detail; subsurface roots are not modeled. Sources: Wildflower Center · A. C. Gibson, Vascular Plants of Williamson County · Kew.
